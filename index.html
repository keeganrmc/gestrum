Gestrum

Air-percussion in the browser. Powered by real-time computer vision and native Web Audio synthesis.

Gestrum translates your physical hand gestures into zero-latency percussion hits in 3D space. Moving your hands in front of your webcam tracks your fingertips as luminous floating orbs. Striking down triggers velocity-sensitive drum hits; holding inside any pad for three seconds locks it into an automated rhythmic loop. No dedicated hardware required, no plugins to install, and zero camera data ever leaves your computer.

Webcam optional — full mouse and touchscreen fallback enabled.

How It Works

The Interaction & Sound Architecture

Designed for immediate tactile response, low cognitive friction, and private client-side performance.

01 / STRIKE

Velocity Downstrokes

MediaPipe tracks 21 skeletal landmarks per hand. Striking downward calculates velocity in real time—gentle taps trigger subtle ghost notes; hard strikes trigger punchy attacks with expansive ripples.

02 / DWELL

3-Second Loop Latch

Hold your fingertip steady inside any pad for 3 seconds. A circular progress ring sweeps around the perimeter, locking the sound into continuous playback synchronized to a 16-step master bar.

03 / SYNTHESIS

Procedural Web Audio

All flagship kits are synthesized procedurally using native Web Audio API oscillators, noise generators, and waveshapers with zero audio file dependencies. Or drag and drop custom WAV/MP3 files.

04 / SCULPT

Spatial Customization

Switch to Layout Mode to drag pads anywhere across your stage. Choose from 5 rounded shapes and scale pad size (which dynamically modulates acoustic shell resonance and pitch).

Privacy Architecture 100% on-device vision via WebAssembly. Video frames are analyzed in temporary memory and never transmitted or recorded.

The Designer

Keegan McConnell

Industrial Design • Human-Centered Physical Interaction • Grand Rapids, MI

“I design products that solve real-world problems and address real user needs.”

I am an industrial design student at Kendall College of Art and Design (KCAD) with an engineering foundation from Michigan State University, currently working as a Technical Illustrator at Haworth. My design approach centers on human friction and physical ergonomics: solving real-world problems through hands-on prototyping, user observation, and thoughtful mechanics.

Gestrum grew out of that same impulse—translating the universal physical instinct of air-drumming into immediate, tactile sound without the friction of specialized hardware, cables, or expensive studio equipment.

Outside of software experiments, my primary work focuses on physical product architecture, ergonomic commercial furniture, and functional hardware.
